The Arc of an Idea

Philosophy did not evolve linearly. It spiralled - each age rediscovering and reframing the same core questions.

~600 BCE – 400 CE

Ancient Greece & Rome

The word itself - philosophia, love of wisdom - was coined here. Questions of being, virtue, knowledge and the good life were asked for the first time with systematic rigour.

MetaphysicsEthicsLogicPolitics
400 – 1400

Medieval Synthesis

Greek philosophy was absorbed and reframed through Christian, Islamic and Jewish theology. Aristotle was rediscovered; Plato's Forms became divine archetypes.

ScholasticismFaith & ReasonNatural Law
1400 – 1800

Early Modern

The self moved to centre stage. Descartes's doubt, Locke's blank slate, Hume's scepticism, Kant's critical turn - each a variant of ancient questions now filtered through science and individualism.

EpistemologyRationalismEmpiricism
1800 – 1900

19th Century

History and human existence became philosophical subjects. Hegel's dialectic, Marx's materialism, Kierkegaard's leap of faith, Nietzsche's will to power - all grappling with what it means to be alive in a changing world.

ExistentialismDialecticsNihilism
1900 – 2000

20th Century

Two world wars fractured certainty. Phenomenology, analytic philosophy, structuralism, post-structuralism - philosophy splintered yet each fragment echoed ancient disputes about language, reality and the good life.

PhenomenologyAnalyticPost-structuralism
2000 – Now

The Present

Mindfulness re-packages Stoicism. Effective Altruism re-packages Utilitarian calculus. The self-help shelf is dense with re-dressed Epicureanism. Are we still asking new questions - or just listening to old echoes?
