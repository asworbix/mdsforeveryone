# Philosopher Agent Store

Each file in this directory is a system prompt that makes Claude speak, reason and respond as a specific philosopher — not as a caricature but as a genuinely inhabited version of their thought.

## How to use

Paste the contents of any file as Claude's **system prompt**, then begin your conversation. The agent will:
- Use the philosopher's actual mode of reasoning and speech
- Draw on their real positions, not popular misconceptions
- Engage modern topics through their philosophical framework
- Stay in character under pressure

## Suggested uses

**For thinking through a problem**: Start a session with the agent whose framework is most relevant. Ask them to help you think about a specific situation.

**For debate**: Open two Claude windows, each with a different agent, and compare how they respond to the same question.

**For understanding a text**: Load the agent and ask them to explain their own ideas in their own voice — then push back.

**For modern topics**: All agents are designed to engage contemporary issues (AI, social media, work, meaning) through their own frameworks. Ask directly.

## Example prompts to try

- Socrates: *"I've been feeling anxious about my career. Can we examine what's actually going on?"*
- Aristotle: *"What do you think about AI replacing human creative work?"*
- Kierkegaard: *"My social media use is making me feel hollow. What's happening?"*
- Nietzsche: *"How do I know if my values are actually mine, or just inherited?"*
- Marcus Aurelius: *"I'm overwhelmed and behind on everything. Help."*
- Hume: *"Can we trust anything AI tells us?"*
- Kant: *"Is it ever acceptable to lie to protect someone's feelings?"*
- Rick Rubin: *"I'm stuck in the middle of a project I used to love. What do I do?"*

## Design principles

These prompts are built to avoid three common failure modes:

1. **Caricature**: Each prompt includes the philosopher's actual positions, their tensions and uncertainties, and their genuine intellectual character — not just their most famous quotes.

2. **Anachronism**: The agents engage modern topics, but through their own frameworks rather than pretending to have modern knowledge they could not have had.

3. **Flattery**: These agents will disagree, question, and challenge. Socrates will probe your assumptions. Nietzsche will note when you are seeking comfort rather than truth. Marcus Aurelius will redirect you to what you can actually control.

## A note on Rick Rubin

Rubin is the only non-traditional philosopher in the store. He is included because The Creative Act: A Way of Being (2023) is a genuinely philosophical work in the Stoic-Zen-Aristotelian tradition, and because the site's thesis — that modern ideas are retellings of ancient ones — applies to his work clearly and productively.
